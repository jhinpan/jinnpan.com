# #24 [Perf] [MI355X] glm53: Select a long row's indexer top-k on up to 16 CTAs
merged 2026-10-25T03:32:17Z 240d58748 +559/-150 files 6

## Motivation

**Summary.** With long contexts, the GLM-5.3 monokernels were slower than the normal path, because the exact top-2048 selection of each indexer row ran on one CTA. This PR selects the top-k of a row of more than 32768 keys on up to 16 CTAs. The result does not change: it is the exact top-2048, as before. On 8x MI355X at TP8, in InferenceX agentic coding with MTP, the monokernels now decode faster than the normal path with MTP: interactivity p50 is 15% higher at concurrency 1, 17% at 2 and 11% at 4. With #15 alone, it was 5%, 5% and 14% lower.

**On main.** This PR is two commits on main (`876ed7f`): `6bfceb6` is `48ff491` rebased with the same changed lines (and `48ff491` is `4f187f6` rebased), and `235bb05` is the fix from the review ("Review fix" below). Apart from that fix, `gfx950/kernel.py`, `layout.py` and the `common` modules they import have the same code as at `4f187f6`, so the results below measured at `4f187f6` and `8068147` still apply. The fix's own results are marked.

**Problem.**

- InferenceX agentic coding (AgentX) sends long requests. In the runs below, the median input of a request is 89k to 285k tokens.
- GLM-5.3 runs the indexer on 21 of its 78 layers. The monokernels run 18 of them (layers 3 to 77 are covered; layers 0 to 2 are dense and use the normal path).
- Without MTP, at sgl-amd-mono `e952b22`, the monokernels lost to the normal path at concurrency 8: interactivity p50 -14%, p90 -42%. With MTP (#15), they lost 5% to 14% at concurrency 1 to 4 (table in "Speed Tests and Profiling").

**Cause.** A debug build (not in this PR) records the start and the end of each stage task of each launch. In an indexer launch:

- The exact top-2048 selection of a row ran on one CTA. That CTA reads all scores of the row at least two times, at about 0.8 ns a key: 211 us a launch at 256k keys.
- The sparse MLA and the MoE of the layer wait for the selection. The other CTAs wait idle.
- The launches without the indexer (57 of the 75 in a step) take the same time at all contexts.

| Keys per row | Rows | Selection (us) | Indexer launch (us) | Launch without indexer (us) | Estimated step (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8k | 1 | 31 | 97 | 53 | 4.79 |
| 256k | 1 | 211 | 325 | 54 | 8.91 |
| 8k | 4 | 27 | 136 | 72 | 6.55 |
| 256k | 4 | 237 | 500 | 72 | 13.11 |

The estimated step is 18 indexer launches and 57 launches without the indexer. From 8k to 256k keys, it grows 4.1 ms at 1 row and 6.6 ms at 4 rows. The measured decode step grows 4.1 ms and 6.3 ms.

## Modifications

- `glm53/gfx950/layout.py`
  - `INDEX_SEL_CHUNK = 4096` (the keys of a select chunk: 512 threads x 8), `INDEX_SEL_PAR_CHUNKS = 8`, `INDEX_SEL_SLICES = 16` and `INDEX_CAND_STAGE_CAP = 16384`.
  - `select_slices(bound)` gives the slices of a row and the chunks of each slice. The kernel and the CPU test call the same function.
  - Three stages before `index_select`: `index_hist` (16 tasks a row), `index_thresh` (1 a row) and `index_gather` (16 a row), and their scratch: about 2.4 MiB a row, 33 MiB a rank for widths 2, 4 and 8.
- `glm53/gfx950/kernel.py`: a row of more than 32768 keys (8 chunks of 4096) selects on up to 16 CTAs of the upper half of the grid (CTAs 128 to 255), each with a run of whole chunks. Before this change, these CTAs waited idle for the selection.
  1. `index_hist`: each slice (CTA) makes a 4096-bin histogram of its chunks in LDS (the top 12 bits of each score key) and writes it to scratch. A release fence orders the histogram before its ready flag.
  2. `index_thresh` runs on the CTA of the row's `index_select`. It adds the histograms of the slices, finds the threshold bin, and publishes the bin, the keys still needed from the bin and the count in the bin.
  3. `index_gather`: each slice writes, in position order, its positions above the bin (winners) and its (key, position) pairs in the bin (candidates) to scratch, and publishes the two counts.
  4. `index_select` copies the winners and the candidates of the slices, in slice order, into the lists that the one-CTA path makes. Then it runs the same radix finish on the candidates.
  - A row of 32768 keys or fewer keeps the one-CTA path: up to 8 chunks, the hand-offs between the CTAs cost what the slices save. When the threshold bin holds more candidates than LDS, the row uses the one-CTA overflow path.
  - The score stage does not change.
  - Review fix: `index_thresh` and `index_select` read the slices' histograms and staged lists, plain stores released before their ready pairs, after an agent-scope acquire, as the `indices` hand-off does. A parallel row whose threshold bin overflows polls its scores in the overflow pass, because its CTA has not read them before.
- `glm53/check.py`, `glm53/spec.py` (review fix): check mode compares each indexer row's selection with the exact top-k of the launch's own scores (score descending, then position): `layer.topk_exact.max_abs`, the keys a row misses, bound 0. Every layer check case runs it.
- Tests
  - `test/unit/test_glm53_select.py` (new): `select_slices` covers each chunk of a row once, in position order, with at most 16 slices and no empty slice (138 cases, 135 distinct bounds); the slice tasks of each width fit in the upper half of the grid.
  - `test/e2e/test_glm53_server.py`: `test_each_covered_layer_past_the_parallel_top_k` runs the layer check on rows of about 108k keys at widths 2 and 4 (27 chunks: 14 slices, 13 of two chunks) and of about 36k keys at width 8 (9 one-chunk slices). It asserts that `past` (65536) is past one chunk a slice. `_check_layers` gets `past` (an indexer check must reach this many keys) and requires `layer.topk_exact` in the log.
  - No test for the acquires: a missing acquire cannot be made to fail deterministically on gfx950.

**No change.** Rows of 32768 keys or fewer, the decode steps of GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek-V4.1 and MiniMax-M3, and the normal path.

## Accuracy Tests

**Setup.** 8x MI355X (gfx950) at TP8; zai-org/GLM-5.3 @ `ba32f8c1` (FP8); the image and the commits in "Speed Tests and Profiling"; the server flags of `test/e2e/glm53_server.py`; a new server for each test.

**Test 1: exact top-k.** A debug build (not in this PR) dumps the scores and the selected positions of each row of a launch. A script computes the exact top-2048 of the dumped scores (score descending, then position ascending) and compares the two sets. The prompts are disjoint windows of real text (SGLang's source). 312 rows (8 ranks; 8k to 256k keys; 1 to 8 rows): 0 rows differ, and all the scores of each row come from the same launch.

**Test 2: E2E tests at TP8 (review fix).** We ran `test/e2e/test_glm53_server.py` as written at `235bb05` on main `876ed7f`. All 7 tests passed in one run (1234 s):

- `TestMonokernelDecode`: average KL 0.015 to 0.025 for each batch (bound 0.06); GSM8K (sgl-eval, thinking on) 0.995.
- `TestMonokernelMtp`: GSM8K 0.995, accept length 3.31.
- `TestMonokernelCheck`: no violations. `layer.topk_exact` and `layer.topk_own` are 0 in all 918 indexer checks.
  - Decode steps: 1425 checks, stream cosine 0.9925 or more, `layer.topk.rel` 0.015 or less.
  - Verify steps: 1200 checks, stream cosine 0.9977 or more, `layer.topk.rel` 0.017 or less.
  - Past the parallel top-k (rows of about 108k keys at widths 2 and 4, about 36k at width 8): 1200 checks, stream cosine 0.9954 or more, `layer.topk.rel` 0.051 or less.
  - `layer.topk.rel` is the share of SGLang's selection that a row misses. It has no bound: SGLang scores with FP8 queries and the kernel with bf16 queries, so keys near the threshold can swap. It is highest in the case whose prompt repeats one paragraph thousands of times; we did not measure why. `layer.topk_exact` checks the selection itself against the launch's own scores.
- `TestMonokernelFault`: passed. Since #55, the test sets `HSA_DISABLE_COREDUMP_ON_EXCEPTION=1` and turns off SGLang's crash dumps itself.

An earlier run of the same file at `8068147`, before the review fix, also passed all 7 tests.

**Test 3: the exact check fails on a wrong selection.** A deliberate error (not in this PR): the slices stage their candidates with the low 20 bits of each key flipped, so the radix finish takes the lowest keys of the threshold bin instead of the highest. Every position stays valid. On the parallel check case, `layer.topk_exact` reports 204 violations (up to 1422 keys of a row's exact top-k missed), and the test fails. No other bound catches it: the lowest stream cosine is 0.991 (bound 0.95), and `layer.topk_own` stays 0. A first version of this control, which dropped the last slice's winners, made the launch publish stale positions and fault on memory instead.

The prompts of the GSM8K and KL tests have fewer than 32768 keys, so these tests run the one-CTA path: they show that this path does not change. Test 1 and the new layer check cover the new path.
